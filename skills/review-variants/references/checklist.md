# Variant checklist

Run every item on every active variant. Skip nothing; write "ok" when an item passes.

1. Claim accuracy. Every factual claim (price, rating, quantity, opening hours, ingredients, delivery) matches the product record or brand facts. Superlatives without a source ("best", "cheapest", "number one") fail.
2. Voice. Tone and personality match the brand book. Check the dos and donts lists line by line. A variant may differ in angle from its siblings but not in voice.
3. Headline. States the angle in one line. Under about 60 characters for screens. No colon-stacked double headlines.
4. Body. One or two sentences that support the headline, not a second headline. Removes nothing the visitor needs to act.
5. CTA. A verb the visitor can do now, tied to the objective ("Book a table", "See the menu", "Get the code"). "Learn more" and "Click here" fail.
6. Chips. Real customer questions, up to six, shared across the campaign. No marketing labels.
7. Legal flags. Health claims, financial promises, comparative claims naming a competitor, unlicensed music references, discounts without conditions, anything aimed at children. Flag; do not rewrite silently.
8. Copy accessibility. Reading level a hurried visitor handles; no all-caps sentences; abbreviations expanded once; the CTA makes sense read alone by a screen reader; no meaning carried only by colour or emoji.
9. Divergence. The variant owns an angle no sibling owns. Two variants on the same angle should be merged or one retired.
10. Surface fit. Where the campaign runs (screen, kiosk, web) the copy still works at that size and dwell time. Screen copy is shorter.

Verdicts: keep (all ok or cosmetic), edit (one or more concrete fixes), retire (fails claim accuracy or legal, or duplicates a sibling with worse numbers).
